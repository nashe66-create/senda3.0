## 1. How to build a *strong* thesis (not a slogan)

A weak thesis sounds like: “People send to more than one person, so grouped checkout wins vs WorldRemit.”

That is not defensible. WorldRemit can add “send again” in a sprint. **Checkout convenience is not a moat.**

A strong thesis must be:

1. **Behavioural** — describes a real recurring job, not a feature wishlist  
2. **Specific** — who, how often, how many obligations, what breaks today  
3. **Product-shaped** — maps to Senda’s Remittance Order / commitments, not generic “send money”  
4. **Falsifiable** — primary research can kill it  
5. **Moat-shaped** — switching cost in a **commitment graph** (people + purposes + cadence), not in FX

### Working strong thesis (v1 — evidence-backed direction, not proven)

> **UK–Africa remitters (especially UK→Nigeria) manage a portfolio of family-support obligations** — multiple recipient *relationships* and multiple *purposes* (household, school fees, health, bills), often on different cadences. Incumbent MTOs optimise the **single transfer**. Senda’s job is to become the **system of record for that portfolio**: plan obligations → fund once when useful → track each payout/purpose independently → reduce admin and purpose anxiety. Defensibility = depth of the saved commitment graph + trust in execution/reconciliation, not cheaper FX.

**What would make this *stronger* after primary research:** prove that in the last 90 days, a material share of UK→NG remitters (a) supported **≥2 distinct recipients**, (b) made **≥2 separate transfers** because of that, (c) experience **admin/purpose pain**, and (d) prefer **one funding event with per-obligation tracking** over N WorldRemit checkouts — *before* seeing Senda branding.

## 4. Multi-relationship & multi-purpose behaviour (core)

### 4.1 Cenfri / insight2impact — strongest UK→SSA quantitative signal

**Study:** *Is there a case for for-purpose remittances?* (Cenfri, Jan 2020). UK digital remittance senders to **Cameroon, Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda**; Mar–Jul 2019.  
**Sample:** n=1,146 quantitative (Nigeria **370**); 24 qualitative (6 per corridor). Partnered with a digital RSP; **not population-representative**.

| Finding | Grade | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 83% send to **three or more recipient groups**; 17% to all listed groups; 92% to ≥1 group | FACT | Groups = parents/grandparents, siblings, friends, extended family, etc. — **not** “3 named people last month” |
| >70% send to friends, siblings, extended family and/or (grand)parents | FACT | Spouses/children less prominent in this digital-sender sample |
| 51% remit to parents/grandparents **≥ once a month** | FACT | Friends much less frequent (19% monthly+) |
| 97% remit for **≥2 purposes**; 92% for **≥3 purposes** | FACT | Multi-select; health, household, celebrations, school fees common |
| Largest yearly amounts: household, school fees, health, bills | FACT | Frequency ≠ value (fees often less frequent, higher value) |
| 71% would use **purpose labelling**; 69% would send **directly to an institution** (school, clinic, utility…) | FACT | Stated preference; pilot needed for revealed preference |
| 24% / 29% said labelling / enforcement would make them send more, more often, or to more people | FACT | Stated; high social-desirability risk |
| Nigeria corridor large in sample (370) and relatively receptive to labelling | FACT | Corridor split in report figures |
| Qualitative: “education for three children, household bills…”; extended-family responsibility; school-fee mistrust → prefer pay school directly | FACT | Quotes in report |

**CAUTION — unit of analysis:** Cenfri proves **multi-relationship + multi-purpose portfolios**, not automatically “I open WorldRemit three times every payday.” That transfer-count behaviour is still a **GAP**.

**INFERENCE for Senda:** The job-to-be-done is closer to **obligation / purpose management** than to “batch FX.” Grouped order is the *mechanism*; **commitments with purposes** are the product.

Sources: [Cenfri report PDF](https://cenfri.org/wp-content/uploads/2020-01-13_For-purpose-remittances-report_final.pdf) · [Infographic](https://cenfri.org/wp-content/uploads/2019-11-12_For-purposes-infographic_final-2.pdf)

### 4.2 UK Nigerian diaspora — “black tax” / obligation weight

**OhentPay 2025 UK–Nigeria Remittance Report** (vendor survey, n≈655 Nigerians in UK) — reported via Techpoint / Condia:

| Finding | Grade | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 51.4% of large transfers go to **mothers**; siblings 12.9%; fathers/extended smaller | FACT (vendor-reported) | Distribution of transfer size by recipient type — not “number of recipients per sender” |
| Extended family more visible on **smaller** transfers | FACT (vendor-reported) | Fits “core + periphery” portfolio |
| 28% say family support affects savings/investment; 15% postponed major life decisions | FACT (vendor-reported) | Supports **commitment burden**, not multi-checkout UX |
| Younger cohort (18–28) often send high income share home | FACT (vendor-reported) | Segment for pain intensity |

**CAUTION:** Competitor/platform survey; methodology not fully public in secondary write-ups; treat as directional.

Sources: [Techpoint summary](https://techpoint.africa/brandpress/ohentpay-data-reveals-black-tax-nigerian-diaspora/) · [Condia summary](https://thecondia.com/ohentpay-nigerians-in-the-uk-remittance-report-2025/)

### 4.3 Somali Londoners — explicit multi-person geometry (older, qualitative+survey)

Lindley (COMPAS): remitting embedded in social relationships; **individual-to-several** (“their list”); just under **¼ regularly** sent to more than one person; many sent to several less frequently in the last year; parents/siblings dominate regular recipients.

| Finding | Grade | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit multi-recipient geometry in UK diaspora remitting | FACT | Somali sample; not Nigerian |
| “List” of dependents as lived practice | FACT | Language maps to Senda commitments |

**CAUTION:** 2000s fieldwork; different community; use as **pattern analogy**, not NG prevalence.

Source: [Lindley COMPAS WP](https://www.compas.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/WP-2007-047-Lindley_Refugee_Diaspora_Remittances.pdf)

### 4.4 Behavioural experiments — purpose control changes sending

De Arcangelis et al. (2015): education **label** raised willingness to remit ~15% among Filipino workers in Rome; enforcement/monitoring added little beyond label.

| Finding | Grade | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose labelling can increase send amounts | FACT (experiment, non-UK/NG) | Supports purpose layer in thesis |
| Product design can kill take-up even when “control” sounds good | FACT | Ambler et al. zero take-up caution from Cenfri lit review |

**INFERENCE:** Senda should treat **purpose tags + optional bill/school direct pay** as thesis amplifiers, not v1 scope creep — but they strengthen moat *direction* beyond grouped checkout.

## 5. What incumbents do / don’t own

| Job | WorldRemit / Remitly / Mukuru today | Senda wedge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move £X to one person fast/cheap | Strong | Weak |
| Remember my 4 dependents + cadences | Weak / address book only | Strong if built |
| One payday funding → several tracked obligations | Weak (repeat transfer) | Strong if researched pain is real |
| Purpose label / pay school directly | Rare / absent (Cenfri: gap) | Differentiator if demanded |
| Corridor licences & liquidity | Strong | Partner / later own rail |

**INFERENCE:** If primary research shows multi-recipient is rare *or* painless (“I just send mum everything”), thesis collapses to price/speed — **do not build**.

## 6. Falsification criteria (use these in primary research)

Kill or park the thesis if, among UK→NG remitters in last 90 days:

1. **<25%** supported ≥2 distinct recipients, **and**  
2. Among multi-recipient remitters, majority say repeating MTO transfers is **easy / not annoying**, **and**  
3. Stated preference for grouped pay+track is weak once they see a concrete flow, **or**  
4. They already consolidate via one conduit person in Nigeria (one recipient, many purposes) and don’t want multi-payout complexity.

Strengthen toward build if:

1. **≥40%** had ≥2 distinct recipients in 90 days, **and**  
2. Median multi-recipient remitters made **≥2 separate provider checkouts** in a typical support month, **and**  
3. Clear admin tools today = WhatsApp/notes/memory, with miss/late/fee-stack stories, **and**  
4. Concept test: prefer one pay + per-person status **before** brand pitch, **and**  
5. Recurring plans (school fees + mum upkeep) appear in the same month often.

*(Thresholds are decision rules for nashe — adjust with sample size; not scientific magic numbers.)*

## 7. Stronger product thesis stack (recommended narrative)

Layer the story so each layer has evidence:

| Layer | Claim | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L1 Obligation portfolio** | Remitters support multiple relationships and purposes | Strong secondary (Cenfri); confirm NG count of *people* in primary |
| **L2 Execution friction** | Portfolio → multiple provider sessions / fee events / tracking mess | **GAP** — primary must measure |
| **L3 System of record** | Value = plan + commitments + history, not the rail | Needs concept test |
| **L4 Purpose control** | Labels / direct-to-institution deepen retention | Strong stated (Cenfri); optional Stage 2 |
| **L5 Distribution** | Win a segment (e.g. UK Nigerian professionals with parent + sibling/school obligations) | Segment via primary |

Do **not** lead with L4 in marketing until L2 is proven — otherwise you look like a research paper, not a remittance app.

## 8. Gaps remaining (honest)

Still **GAP** after this synthesis (same as Sept evidence gap, now sharper):

1. % of UK→**Nigeria** remitters with ≥2 **distinct** recipients in a defined window  
2. Typical recipient count and **number of separate transfers** per cycle  
3. Whether one “conduit” recipient absorbs multi-purpose sends (kills multi-payout need)  
4. Admin tools and pain intensity (WhatsApp, banking apps, memory)  
5. Trust to let a new brand execute N payouts from one payment  
6. Willingness to pay for workflow (not asked yet — correctly deferred)

**Primary field work is required.** Instruments: [primary-research-protocol.md](./primary-research-protocol.md).

**Constraint this run:** secondary research + protocol authored in-store; **live UK remitter interviews/survey fielding need nashe recruitment** (communities, diaspora Slack/WhatsApp, Prolific with screeners, church/alumni networks). Agent cannot ethically invent respondents.
