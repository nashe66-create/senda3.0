# Senda Corridor Screen — September 2026

## Scope

Current screening universe: UK → Nigeria, Kenya, Zambia, South Africa, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda and Ghana.

The purpose is to identify corridors that deserve customer validation, not to declare a final market winner. Senda's current product hypothesis is: one Remittance Order, one customer payment, multiple independently tracked recipient payouts.

## Flutterwave infrastructure screen

Flutterwave's current transfer documentation lists Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, South Africa, Rwanda and Zambia among supported payout countries. Bank-transfer documentation lists NGN, GHS, KES, TZS, UGX, ZAR and ZMW, and provides country-specific transfer guides including Rwanda. Mobile-money support is documented for Ghana, Kenya, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda and Zambia. Flutterwave supports single and bulk transfers from available balance. UK collection/payment capability and the exact commercial structure for a UK remittance business require direct confirmation with Flutterwave.

## World Bank / Migration Observatory cost evidence

World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide Q3 2025 corridor averages:

| Corridor | Average total cost | Key payout/infrastructure observation |
|---|---:|---|
| UK → Nigeria | 1.26% | Bank/cash; individual providers can be materially cheaper |
| UK → Kenya | 2.63% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash are represented |
| UK → Zambia | 2.89% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash represented |
| UK → South Africa | 4.64% | Bank/cash; relatively high average cost |
| UK → Tanzania | 4.35% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash represented |
| UK → Rwanda | 7.43% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash represented; wide provider dispersion |
| UK → Ghana | 3.79% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash represented |
| UK → Uganda | 3.66% | Bank, mobile wallet and cash represented |

These are dated benchmark observations, not live quotes. Provider-level pricing varies substantially.

## Market evidence

The Migration Observatory's 2025 briefing, using World Bank RPW data, reported average costs for sending £120 from the UK in 2024 of: Nigeria 6.5%, Kenya 5.4%, Zambia 7.9%, South Africa 7.2%, Uganda 5.0%, Tanzania 5.7%, Rwanda 7.9% and Ghana 5.1%. The briefing also reported that around 27% of people born abroad sent remittances in 2021/22, with migrants from Africa and the Middle East among the groups most likely to remit.

Bilateral UK destination figures are limited: the Migration Observatory notes that official bilateral remittance estimates after 2021 are not available in the same form. Historical 2021 estimates included Nigeria £2.76bn, Kenya £1.02bn, South Africa £0.??bn and Ghana £0.668bn; use the underlying source before quoting the South Africa value in external materials.

## Provider landscape

Across the screened corridors, the established customer alternatives include WorldRemit, Remitly, Wise, Western Union, MoneyGram, Sendwave and/or Mukuru depending on destination. Current provider pages show bank deposit, cash pickup and mobile-wallet options on several routes. This means Senda should not rely on basic speed, safety, tracking or low-price claims as its sole differentiation.

## Senda-specific opportunity hypothesis

The differentiated problem remains workflow rather than basic transfer execution:

1. A customer supports more than one person regularly.
2. Those commitments may have different amounts, purposes and payout methods.
3. Existing remittance products are generally structured around one transfer/order at a time, even where a provider can support multiple transfers operationally.
4. Senda can group commitments into one Remittance Order, collect once, then track each payout independently.
5. The core customer evidence still missing is the prevalence and importance of multi-recipient behaviour.

## Corridor observations

### UK → Nigeria
- Strong strategic fit for the current pilot.
- Flutterwave explicitly supports NGN bank transfers and Nigeria is a core supported transfer market.
- CBN has an active IMTO framework and in March 2026 reminded IMTOs of revised requirements, including designated naira settlement accounts with authorised dealer banks.
- Competition is strong and pricing can be very low, so Senda should lead with grouped commitments and recipient management rather than cheaper FX.
- Primary research priority: very high.

### UK → Kenya
- Strong infrastructure fit: KES bank transfers plus M-Pesa support.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 2.63%; provider-level observations include substantially lower costs.
- Kenya has a substantial UK diaspora and a formal money-remittance licensing framework.
- Primary research priority: high.

### UK → Zambia
- Flutterwave supports ZMW bank transfer and mobile money (MPS).
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 2.89%, while the Migration Observatory's 2024 average was much higher at 7.9%, showing meaningful variation by method/provider and time.
- Mukuru and WorldRemit provide established alternatives including mobile wallet/cash options.
- Primary research priority: medium-high; validate whether the smaller UK diaspora is offset by higher multi-recipient/recurring support behaviour.

### UK → South Africa
- Flutterwave supports ZAR bank transfer.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 4.64%; 2024 Migration Observatory average was 7.2%.
- Competition includes Wise, WorldRemit, Mukuru, Ria, MoneyGram and Western Union.
- South Africa has a large UK-linked migrant population, but customer use cases may include more sophisticated bank-to-bank transfers rather than family-support remittances.
- Primary research priority: medium; segment by purpose before investing heavily.

### UK → Uganda
- Flutterwave supports UGX bank transfers and Airtel/MTN mobile money.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 3.66%; Migration Observatory 2024 average was 5.0%.
- WorldRemit and Mukuru have established UK-to-Uganda offerings.
- Primary research priority: medium-high if research identifies multi-recipient family support.

### UK → Tanzania
- Flutterwave supports TZS bank transfers and Airtel, HaloPesa, Tigo and Vodacom mobile-money rails.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 4.35%; Migration Observatory 2024 average was 5.7%.
- WorldRemit and Remitly support bank, cash and mobile-wallet delivery.
- Primary research priority: medium-high.

### UK → Rwanda
- Flutterwave documents Rwanda payout support and mobile money via M-Pesa and MTN.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 7.43%, with very wide provider-level dispersion.
- Rwanda's regulator lists Flutterwave Rwanda as an e-money issuer and lists several licensed remittance providers, indicating a developed regulated payments environment.
- Primary research priority: medium; higher cost creates a potential customer pain point, but the addressable UK population is smaller.

### UK → Ghana
- Flutterwave supports GHS bank transfers and mobile-money options including AirtelTigo, MTN and Telecel.
- World Bank Q3 2025 average cost was 3.79%; Migration Observatory 2024 average was 5.1%.
- Strong provider competition and broad payout choice make the basic transfer proposition crowded.
- Primary research priority: high enough to test because of diaspora/remittance relevance and strong mobile-money infrastructure.

## Regulatory principle

Senda should not assume that using Flutterwave removes Senda's regulatory obligations. FCA guidance says receiving customer money before passing it to another party may constitute a regulated payment service, including money remittance, and providing payment services as a regular business generally requires appropriate FCA authorisation/registration unless an exclusion applies. Country-specific destination regulation must also be assessed. The preferred structure should therefore be established with Flutterwave and specialist UK regulatory counsel before customer funds are handled live.

## Research conclusion

The secondary research supports continuing with UK → Nigeria as the immediate validation corridor. It also supports testing Kenya and Ghana, with Uganda, Tanzania and Zambia as additional candidates and South Africa/Rwanda as secondary hypotheses. This is not a final commercial ranking: the missing variable is customer behaviour around multiple recipients and recurring commitments.

The next research stage should therefore be a short behavioural survey and a small number of interviews targeted at people who actually remit from the UK to the shortlisted destinations.
